# One scene across a page of buttons

A page of 1×1 buttons can show a single picture that moves across button borders — a fish tank, a sky that runs through a day, a synthwave sunset. It is the most striking thing Desmaptic does, and it rests on four ideas. `assets/scene_template.zsh` implements all of them; read this first to know why.

Use it when the user asks for a scene, an ambient page, "something beautiful across the whole page". It is a showpiece: every cell glides all the time, so say that it is meant to be looked at, not left running all day. For the same reason the rules for faces that move all the time hold in every tile (`svg-craft.md` → Keeping the phone smooth). Flat-colored `rect`s, `circle`s and `ellipse`s and gradient-filled square-cornered `rect`s (a sky band, a line drawn as a thin rect) go anywhere, still or moving, however far past the window they reach. Every `path`, `polygon`, `polyline` and `line` stays within one window and keeps its shape: a mountain range or a seabed drawn across the whole world is cut into one piece per window, large areas are `rect`s, shapes move whole, by `transform`. A line drawn across buttons with wide strokes (a glowing curve, a beam) has at most two wide strokes on any button it crosses, and a stroke that must not show is transparent (`stroke-opacity="0"`), not pushed out of the window. Gradient strokes, and gradients on a `path`, `circle` or `ellipse` that changes or moves under a world-space gradient, go only on a few things — the template's sun halo and its three clouds are fine; gradients on every mover are not. Exact limits: `get_available_actions` topic `svg` → `widgetFaces.budget` and `widgetFaces.scenes`.

## 1. Every button is a window onto one world

Give the scene one coordinate system — say 100 units per button — and give each button a viewBox that looks at its own part of it, spaced as the buttons stand on screen, gap included:

```
gap = 100 × cellGap / cellSize      (GET /api/view; no answer → 12.5, the phone's 10 pt gap on 80 pt cells)
cell at world column cx, world row cy (0 = bottom)  →  viewBox="{cx*(100+gap)} {(rows-1-cy)*(100+gap)} 100 100"
```

**Use the template's `window(cx, cy)`** — it returns that box, with the gap the loop takes from `/api/view` every tick — rather than writing the numbers yourself; size the world from it too (`window(7, 0)[2]` is the world's width). A script in zsh or awk computes `gap` once per tick from the same answer.

Never lay the windows edge to edge (`cx*100`): on the phone a gap stands between two buttons, but the world would have none, so a mover crossing a border is drawn on both sides of the gap at once — its halves a gap apart, one cut short and the other sticking out too far, a tilted or curved one at two heights. With the gap in the world, the part of a mover over the gap is hidden as if behind a window frame, and the two halves line up. This holds for a row of buttons too, not only a full page.

Write **every** element and every gradient in world coordinates, gradients with `gradientUnits="userSpaceOnUse"`. The viewBox clips each window, so a sky gradient, a sun's halo or a reflection on water runs across the borders without a seam: no shape needs cutting for the picture's sake. For the phone's sake, a `path`, `polygon`, `polyline` or `line` longer than its window is still cut into one piece per window (the rules above); flat `rect`s, `circle`s and `ellipse`s and square-cornered gradient `rect`s never need it. Send `fit: "stretch"`, so each window fills its cell edge to edge.

**The grid turns with the phone.** A scene is wider than tall, so it is meant for landscape: a portrait page of 4 columns × 8 rows becomes 8 columns × 4 rows. The mapping is: world column = the button's portrait `row` (0…7), world row = its portrait `col` (0…3), and **world row 0 is the bottom**. Create the page and its script in ONE `add_pages_to_client` call: give every cell a `ref` and write `{{CELL_ID:<ref>}}` in the scene script (the tool puts in the UUIDs before sending), or build the table `(cellId, row, col)` from the result's `buttons` — never guess it.

**Better: ask the phone where the buttons stand.** `GET /api/view` lists every cell on screen in `cells` — `id`, `row`, `col`, `rowSpan`, `colSpan` on the upright page (as the config stores them, the same in landscape) and `galleryItem` — with the upright grid on screen `columns` × `rows` (4×8 phone grid, 6×8 pad grid — an iPad, an iPhone Duo unfolded), `orientation`, and `cellSize` / `cellGap` — one cell's side and the gap between cells, in points. In landscape a cell at (`row`, `col`) is drawn at screen column `row` and screen row `(columns − 1) − col − (colSpan − 1)` from the top — the mapping above. Taking positions from there each tick, the scene follows a button the user moved, the iPad's two extra columns and a turn of the screen, and can draw a tall world upright and a wide one turned (the Gallery's aquarium: a deep tank upright, a reef sideways). Space the windows by the `gap` above, from the same answer, so a fish crossing a gap is hidden behind it just as long as it takes to swim across (windows laid edge to edge make everything jump the gap). Draw only while `cells` holds the script's own `{{CELL_ID}}` — then its page is the one on screen. An older phone app sends no `cells`: keep the table as the fallback, or draw nothing.

**An iPad may show several deck windows side by side**, each on a page of its own. The top-level `cells`, `columns`, `rows`, `orientation`, `cellSize` and `cellGap` are then the window the user touched last; `windows` lists every window on screen (that one first), each with its own `pageId`, `visibleCellIds`, `cells` and grid (`columns`, `rows`, `isLandscape` — `true` for a grid on its side —, `cellSize`, `cellGap`). Look for the script's own `{{CELL_ID}}` in `windows[].cells` and lay out by that window's fields, read from that window's object rather than the first match anywhere in the body, so the scene keeps moving while the user works in the window beside it; with no `windows` key (an older app) use the top-level fields.

## 2. Each window keeps a constant structure

A glide needs the same elements in every frame of a cell. So decide **statically**, per cell, what it contains: an element belongs to a cell if the region it can *ever* occupy touches that cell's rectangle — not if it happens to be there now. Backgrounds go in every cell; mountains only in the rows they span; a ring only in the cells around it; a depth line only in the cells it crosses. This also keeps frames small (3–5 KB a cell).

Movers that roam the whole world (a fish, a cloud) belong to every cell they can reach. When few cells need them at a time, *park* them instead: outside that cell's window, collapsed, `opacity="0"` — present in the structure, invisible. Parking is a jump (§3): a mover comes into a cell through one frame at its real place with `opacity="0"`, and leaves the same way — a glide straight from the parking spot would fly it in across the cell.

A parked or hidden twin is the same element with other numbers. Copy the live element exactly: its `id` and tag, the path's command letters and point count, the paint kinds of fill **and** stroke (the twin of a stroked shape carries a stroke, the twin of a gradient-filled one the gradient), linecap, linejoin, the number of dash entries, `fill-rule` and clip. One mismatch and the whole cell cross-fades every time the mover comes or goes. An outline that comes and goes stays in every frame, hidden with `stroke-opacity="0"`.

## 3. The invisible jump

Anything that loops — a cloud leaving the right edge and re-entering on the left, a floor line reaching the viewer and restarting at the horizon — must not be seen travelling back. A glide interpolates *every* change, including the jump home, so the mover would fly backwards through the picture.

Make the jump while it cannot be seen, in three frames:
1. **Hide it where it is.** Where it left — off-world, or behind something — set `opacity="0"` and change nothing else: it does not move in this frame.
2. **Move it while hidden.** The next frame (or a later one) puts it at the start, still `opacity="0"`.
3. **Show it.** A later frame makes it visible again where it is, and it carries on.

Never move it in the frame that hides it. The phone starts every glide from what is on screen, and a fade is not over when the next frame comes: with a glide longer than the period, part of it still shows. A frame that keeps the element at `opacity="0"` while it moves cuts what is left of the fade — the element disappears at once and moves unseen — so the three frames are safe at any glide length. Moved in the hiding frame, it is glided to its new place while it fades: a faint ghost slides across the picture. Showing it again in place, without a move, is always fine. Hidden means transparent — `opacity`, `fill-opacity` / `stroke-opacity` or the color's own alpha, and for a `<text>` its `opacity` alone; outside the window or at zero size is not hidden. The exact rule: `get_available_actions` topic `svg` → `widgetFaces.svgReference.recipes` → "invisible jump (hide, move, show)".

The cut comes with any move of a hidden element — its position, size, turn, clip or dash numbers, or the transform of a `<g>` around it — also one that simply goes on: a cloud that keeps drifting as it fades out at dusk loses the rest of its fade at the next frame. To fade out something that keeps moving, hold its numbers still for a few frames after the one that sets `opacity="0"`: then the fade is over, and the phone ends it by itself. How many frames for your glide length: `get_available_actions` topic `svg` → `widgetFaces.svgReference.recipes` → "ticker / spinner (continuous motion)".

Parking a mover (§2) runs on these frames: it is shown only in a frame after the one that brought it to its real place, and on the way out it is hidden where it is and parked in a later frame — once its fade is over if it was still in sight, since parking is a move and cuts the rest of the fade.

Drive all lanes from one counter so their spacing never drifts: a lane of `n` steps has `n + 3` slots — the visible run `0, 1/n, …, 1` with both ends where the mover cannot be seen (off-world), then "hidden at 1" (step 1) and "hidden at 0" (step 2); the slot after them is the run's first, `0` shown (step 3). Count whole slots and divide (`slot / n`), not a float step that lands on 0.9999… `cycle()` in the template does exactly this.

Bodies that set and rise (a sun, a moon) are better kept on their arc **below** the horizon than parked: whatever hides them there (the sea, the world's edge) does the job, and nothing ever jumps.

## 4. One loop, one process, steady frames

One script on one button of the page posts to all cells over a single keep-alive connection (`http.client`, not a new `curl` per frame). Generating 32 frames in Python takes a couple of milliseconds; the template builds a tick's frames first and then posts them back to back, well under 100 ms for all of them, so the windows change together. Whether all of a tick's posts always reach the phone at the same moment is not verified: the template waits for each answer before the next post, and the agent passes them on one by one. A few may come an instant later now and then — invisible in a slow scene, a seam that parts for an instant by a fast mover.

Period 2 s, `easing: "linear"`, `duration` 1.2–1.35 × the period (the template: 1.2): a glide still running when the next frame comes is taken over at the same speed, so the motion never pauses, even when Wi-Fi brings a frame up to ~0.15 s late.

**Every tile that shows something moving moves on one clock.** Each one gets a frame at every tick, with the same `duration` and `easing`. Each cell takes over its own glide from what it shows, so a tile with another glide length, or one posted a tick later, draws its half of a mover apart from the other half at the seam.

A tile that holds only slow background, with no mover in it, may get a frame every few ticks, with a glide as long as that interval × the same factor — it cuts the traffic, not the look. It goes back to every tick as soon as a mover can reach it before its next frame, and on the tick it turns slow it gets its first long frame at once — otherwise its last short glide ends and the background stops for a moment. A cell whose drawing does not change needs no frames at all.

**An entering mover leads.** With a glide k × the period, the phone shows every mover (k − 1) of a step behind its frames — all tiles alike, so the seams hold. But a mover that comes into a tile starts there from the hidden frame's place, ahead of where the neighbouring tile shows it, so it appears a little ahead of its other half and slides back into it. For a fast mover, draw the entering frame where the phone shows the mover when the next frame comes: the previous on-screen place + (new place − previous on-screen place) × period / duration, the on-screen place carried from tick to tick (each glide starts where the last one had got to). A slow mover does not need it.

**`"dropped": true` while the phone app is in front** (`isForeground: true` in `GET /api/view`) means the scene costs more than the phone can draw (or, away from home, more than the link carries) — with a current phone app at home that is almost every `dropped`, as a page in the background or off screen gets `"visible": false` instead. The loop still sends less while answers carry it, its glides stretched to the slower pace, as every loop does (`live-widgets.md`; the template does), but the fix is a cheaper scene: fewer path shapes, shapes cut to each window, fewer tiles that move. A slower tick by design does not help: every cell still glides all the time, and the motion only gets jerkier. `postsLastMinute` falling in `get_startup_script_status` is the same sign.

**The template is python, so it needs the Command Line Tools.** `python3` is not on every Mac: without the tools `/usr/bin/python3` is a stub that opens an install dialog each time it runs, and the agent restarts a failed script every minute. So the template begins with a few zsh lines that look first — `xcode-select -p`, never `command -v`, which finds the stub — and, without the tools, exit with the reason on stderr (the phone lists it among its errors); with them they hand the rest of the file to that `python3` (`exec "$dev/usr/bin/python3" -I - <<'PY'`). Keep those lines when you adapt it, tell the user the scene needs the Command Line Tools (`xcode-select --install`) on every Mac it should run on, and write python only between `<<'PY'` and the closing `PY`. Its own checks run the same way: `zsh scene_template.zsh --dump out.json`.

A tap on any button of the page appends a line to a file under `$DESMAPTIC_STORAGE/<scene>/`; the loop notices the file grew and switches mode, theme or speed. Same command on all 32 buttons.

## Saved faces for the page

Give every cell a light saved face (`svgFace`, `svgFaceFit: "stretch"`): the static scenery of its window — background, ground, anything that does not move. The page is then complete when the agent is not connected, and the scene fades in over it. Leave out what moves and what is heavy (a grid of lines): 32 faces add up in the synced config, keep the page well under 50 KB.

## Look before you ship

You cannot judge a scene from its source. `render_preview` draws one button per call — pass `fit: "stretch"`; a 100×100 viewBox is drawn as a 1×1 button: render a few neighbouring windows — a corner, a seam between rows, the busiest cell — at two or three moments of the scene's cycle, and pass consecutive frames as `svg` + `nextFrame` to see the motion between them. With shell access, also tile all 32 windows into one picture (nested `<svg x y width height viewBox>` per window, rendered by `qlmanage -t`) — that is the browser engine, good for composition and seams; details such as text order and dashes only `render_preview` shows as the phone draws them. Things only the picture shows: a stripe that has the same color as what it crosses, a ribbon with a hard cut edge, a sun that sets behind a mountain instead of into the sea, a glow that stops at a row because the next row did not include it.

Neither `render_preview` nor `view_live_button` replays how the phone takes over a glide that is still running: each draws a glide from the frame before as if it had finished. A move made in the hiding frame (§3) they do show: pass that frame and the one before it as `svg` + `nextFrame` (or find it in `view_live_button`'s strip), and a fading copy slides across in the in-betweens. Halves that part at a seam and an entering mover that leads (§4) show only on the phone — check those rules in your code, frame by frame. A cross-fade both do show, and the agent's answer to a post whose frame cross-fades names the first difference in `crossFade`.
